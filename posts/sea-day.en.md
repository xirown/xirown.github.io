I woke up and the window was unusually bright. The list of things to do was still in my head, but my hands wouldn't move. I learned last year that on days like this, forcing myself to sit at the desk leaves nothing behind. So I put a hand warmer and a bottle of water in my bag and went to the sea.

## Getting there

When you get off the bus, you still can't see the sea. You turn down two alleys, pass a low wall, and only then does the smell of water arrive. That moment is honestly the best part. The feeling that nothing has started yet.

The wind was stronger than I expected, so I wound my scarf twice. The waves came in slowly from far away and fell apart at my feet without any strength. The sound stays the same, and yet it's a little different each time you listen.

## Practising doing nothing

Usually I acted like someone who had to be doing something. Even while moving, even while eating. In front of the sea that doesn't work. Not because there's nothing I can do, but because I felt I didn't have to.

- Counting three times how far the water comes in before it goes back
- Checking whether the boat far away is moving
- Putting my cold hands in my pockets, then taking them out again

Nothing like a grand meditation. Just things like that.

> I'd like about an hour a day of having done nothing at all.

I thought that on the bus home. Then I fell asleep right away. So I really did nothing.

## On the way back

After washing up at home and sitting down, I could see things that weren't visible in the morning. That the work I'd put off wasn't actually that much. That yesterday's worry wasn't as urgent as it felt. The sea didn't give me an answer, but it let me measure the size of the problem again.

I'll go again on the next hard day. With gloves this time.

## What I brought home

On the way back I stopped at a small shop and bought a warm drink. Holding the cup, my fingertips slowly loosened. At home I found a handful of sand in my pocket. In my shoes too. I didn't mind while cleaning up, because that sand felt like proof that I'd been to the sea today.

## Next time

Next time I'll bring a mat and gloves. And arrive before sunset, to see how the light changes on the water. I'd like to take a photograph, but I don't want to be so busy taking it that I miss the moment. One picture is enough.
