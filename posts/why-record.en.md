Sometimes I read notes from this time last year. Things I underlined because they mattered so much are now barely memorable. Other times a line I jotted down carelessly stays for years. Which means I don't get to decide how long a record lives.

## Memory slips more than we think

They say people don't store memories, they rebuild them each time. So the same event comes back differently depending on your mood. That frightened me a little. It means a good part of what I lived through may no longer be mine.

A record fills that gap. The weather that day, the clothes I was wearing, a single sentence. Details are the thread that pulls memory back. So I try to write down facts first. Feelings can be attached later.

## Material that explains who I am now

When I have to explain myself in an interview or an introduction, I always freeze. But if I dig through my notes, the material is there. What I was worried about, what I gave up, what I found fun.

> A record isn't about holding on to the past; it's about giving the present me some evidence.

So these days I try to leave writing that is frequent rather than good. Unfinished sentences are fine. The only person who will read it later is the future me.

## Not every day, but often

I failed at writing daily about three times. So now I've settled on this.

- When something comes to mind, put it in the notes app first.
- Once a week, skim the notes and turn them into a post.
- Don't delete the notes that never became a post.

It's too loose to call a rule, but this is the kind that lasts. What lasts ends up being the best method.

## Why I don't delete

Sometimes an old note embarrasses me and I want to erase it. What was I thinking, writing that? But I don't delete it. Once I start tidying the past by present standards, all that's left is a well-packaged record.

> An embarrassing record is still a record. Those are the ones that prove more useful later.

Evidence of growth doesn't come from a list of things done well; it comes from reading a sentence you used to write and finding it a little unfamiliar now. If that's true, an awkward old note isn't a reason to erase it — it's a reason to keep it.
