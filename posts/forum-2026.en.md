For the past few years, Korean universities have seen repeated cases of exam cheating with generative AI — in midterms and finals, in online exams, and now, reportedly, in professional licensing exams and the 2027 college entrance exam (CSAT).

Universities have mostly responded by holding a retake. At the same time they promote themselves as "AI-leading" campuses and encourage students to use AI in coursework, while leaving assessment standards and consequences for misuse untouched. Behind all of this sits an exam culture built for easy grading — multiple-choice and short-answer tests, recycled question banks, and a long-standing trade in "jokbo," student-made answer archives that substitute for actual study.

We think stronger invigilation is not the answer. The deeper question is why our universities and our society still rank people by test scores at all: a competitive, meritocratic order that culminates in the CSAT and in credentialism, and that teaches students that getting through the exam is all that matters.

## What we are asking for

- **A different kind of assessment.** Move away from multiple-choice and rote recall toward oral exams, written essays, and process-based evaluation, so that students who genuinely understand the material are the ones who are rewarded.
- **Real consequences for cheating.** Codify strong sanctions — including revocation of degrees and academic records — for misconduct in university exams, admissions interviews and essays, and national licensing exams, including the CSAT.
- **An end to the double standard.** If universities encourage AI use, they must also set standards for it and take responsibility for the consequences.
- **An end to jokbo culture.** Stop relying on recycled questions and student answer archives as a substitute for teaching and assessment.
